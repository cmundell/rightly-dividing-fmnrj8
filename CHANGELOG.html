3.9.0Discipleship and study methods October 22, 20262 new3 changed1 accuracy
  • New
    Discipleship paths (Today, or Go > Discipleship paths): three paths of eight steps - First Steps (assurance, God's Word, prayer, baptism, the local church, telling others, temptation, walking in the Spirit), Growing in Christ (abiding, grace, humility, love, forgiveness, holiness, serving, His coming) and Leading Your Family (teaching your children, choosing to serve the LORD, husbands and wives, children and fathers, from a child, praying for your children, a home of grace, telling the next generation). Each step: Read (with Listen), Learn (a short teaching and a memory verse), Study (a study method to practise on the passage), Talk (questions, and something to talk over together) and Do (one thing for the week). Progress is kept; Print a step to meet with a friend, a mentor, or your husband or wife.
  • New
    Study methods (Today, Go > Study methods, or Ctrl+K): guided worksheets that teach how to study a passage - Observe, Interpret, Apply; SOAP; word study; character study; topical study; verse mapping; book overview. The app fills in study aids beside the worksheet: repeated words, cross-references, the Hebrew and Greek words with their meanings and other verses that use them, the people in the passage and their families, Nave's topics, the verse broken into phrases, and a book's timeline events and places. Worksheets are saved in Notes > Journal and can be printed with room to write.
  • Changed
    The Daily button is now called Devotional (Spurgeon's Morning and Evening), in the toolbar, the Go menu, the tour and Today.
  • Changed
    Dark mode: what you type in notes, the journal, prayers and worksheets is now white so it stands out; saved notes in the Notes tab too. Light and sepia are unchanged.
  • Changed
    Single-chapter references to the Psalms read “Psalm 23”, not “Psalms 23”, in family devotions and the paths.
  • Accuracy
    The paths are built by a script that checks every reference is a real KJV verse and that every word shown in quotation marks appears word for word in that step's verses; it will not build otherwise. The teaching, questions and actions are new wording, offered for review.
  • Tests
    A new 34-check test run covers the paths, every study method and its aids, saving to the journal, the Devotional name and dark-mode note text; all earlier tests still pass.

3.6.0Map detail and the Red Sea crossing October 22, 20263 new1 changed2 fixed1 accuracy
  • New
    Terrain on every map: mountains, valleys, deserts and the Jordan rift, from NASA's Blue Marble shaded-relief imagery (public domain). The Terrain button turns it on and off; the choice is remembered.
  • New
    Key places always marked for context: Jerusalem on every map, plus Damascus, Babylon, Nineveh, Rome, Athens, Alexandria and Antioch. More appear as you zoom in: 26 more cities and mountains at country scale, 28 more up close (Bethany, Cana, Shiloh, mount of Olives...), regions (Egypt, Assyria, Galilee, Judaea, Moab...), seas (the Great Sea, the Red Sea, the Salt Sea...) and rivers (Nile, Jordan, Euphrates, Tigris). Click a marked city for every verse that names it.
  • Changed
    Red Sea crossing redrawn to match Scripture: Israel turns back from Etham “in the edge of the wilderness” (Exodus 13:20; 14:2), camps at Pi-hahiroth by the sea, and crosses “through the midst of the sea upon the dry ground” (14:22) to the wilderness of Shur on the far shore (15:22), three days from Marah. Placement follows the traditional view (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, M. G. Kyle: Pi-hahiroth within 10 miles of modern Suez). Etham and the wilderness of Shur use OpenBible.info's sites that fit this route (Tell Abu Sefeh; the area east of the gulf). The map zooms in on the crossing, and the card explains where each place is drawn and why.
  • New
    Crossings on dry ground (the Red Sea, the Jordan in Joshua 3-4) are drawn as a sandy path between two walls of water.
  • Accuracy
    New build check: every crossing must actually pass through water (the sea or the Jordan). Stops drawn at a candidate site or a published source are listed by the build and explained in the app.
  • Fixed
    Map labels were drawn too small on the wide “Region” view.
  • Fixed
    A sea label could appear twice or be cut off at the map's edge.
  • Credits
    NASA Earth Observatory (Blue Marble), via FreeTiler; International Standard Bible Encyclopedia.

    122 automated checks pass (16 new).

  • New
    Places tab > Journeys: 25 Bible journeys on the map. Paul: conversion and early years; first, second and third missionary journeys; voyage to Rome.   • New
    Play / pause, step buttons, slider, left/right arrow keys, or click any stop. The route draws itself leg by leg.
  • New
    Each stop shows the KJV verse that names it, a short note, the distance of the leg and the running total, and how it was travelled (over land, by sea, through the water, or not described).
  • New
    “All together” shows all of one person's journeys at once.
  • New
    “Follow my reading”: the map keeps pace with the chapter you are reading. The chapter map says when a chapter is part of a journey.
  • New
    Short hops (e.g. Bethany to Gethsemane) zoom in automatically; long legs zoom back out.
  • Accuracy
    The journey data is rebuilt and checked by a script that refuses to save it if any check fails:
    • every stop's name must appear word for word in its KJV verse
    • every location must be the one OpenBible.info gives for that verse
    • every quotation in the notes must match the KJV text
    • sea legs must stay on water and land legs on land

    Corrections made while checking: 1 Kings 18:20 (mount Carmel, not Carmel in Judah); wilderness of Maon; 8 misquotes; and 6 notes fixed after reading every cited verse (e.g. Luke's sermon “in the plain”; Philip and Nathanael called in Galilee, not at Bethabara). Uncertain sites (Mount Sinai, Exodus campsites, Emmaus, the country of the Gergesenes, etc.) are marked “Location uncertain”. Order of events in the Gospels follows A. T. Robertson's Harmony of the Gospels (1922).

  • Fixed
    Dragging the slider while a journey was playing snapped back to the previous stop.
